# Claims Handling Guideline Water Damage

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Claims Handling Guideline Water Damage |
| **As of** | 1 March 2026 |
| **Owner** | Claims Policy Team, Nordlicht Insurance |
| **Valid from** | 1 March 2026 |

This guideline replaces the version valid from 1 July 2023.

## Summary

A new claims handling guideline for water damage has been in force since 1 March 2026.
For water damage claims above €3,000, an assessor must inspect the damage before payout
(before that the threshold was €10,000). This extends the processing of these claims by
about two to three weeks.

## Purpose

This guideline describes how water damage claims are handled at Nordlicht Insurance.
It applies to all water damage claims reported on or after 1 March 2026, in the products
"Residential Building Comfort" and "Home Contents Plus".

## What changed on 1 March 2026

| | Until 28 February 2026 | Since 1 March 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Assessor mandatory for water damage claims | above €10,000 estimated amount | above €3,000 estimated amount |

Nothing else changed. The threshold for the other damage types stays as it was.

## The rule

For every water damage claim with an estimated amount above €3,000, an assessor must inspect
the damage on site before any payout is approved.

- The claims handler orders the assessor as soon as the estimated amount is known.
- The waiting time for an assessor appointment is about two weeks.
- After the visit, the assessor delivers the report within a few working days.
- The claim is only decided once the report is available.

Water damage claims up to €3,000 are handled as before: the claims handler decides based on
photos, invoices and the repair quote. No assessor is needed.

## Why the threshold was lowered

A review of water damage claims between €3,000 and €10,000 showed two problems:

- Hidden moisture in walls and floors was often missed when the damage was judged from photos only.
  This led to follow-up damage and a second claim.
- Repair quotes in this range differed widely for comparable damage.

An inspection on site finds hidden moisture early and gives a reliable basis for the payout.

## Effect on processing time

The assessor step extends the processing of the affected claims by about two to three weeks.

| Water damage claim | Typical processing time |
|---|---|
| Up to €3,000, no assessor | around 18 days |
| Above €3,000, with assessor | around 5 to 6 weeks |

A little more than half of all water damage claims are above €3,000. The average processing time for
water damage claims therefore rises noticeably from March 2026 onwards. This increase is
expected and is a result of this guideline, not of slower work in the claims team.

## Exceptions

- **Emergency measures:** drying and securing the property may start immediately and do not
  need to wait for the assessor.
- **Advance payment:** for claims that take longer, an advance payment can be made before
  the claim is closed.
