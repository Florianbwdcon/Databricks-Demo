# Event Report Frost January 2026

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Event Report Frost January 2026 |
| **As of** | 6 February 2026 |
| **Owner** | Claims Operations, Nordlicht Insurance |
| **Valid from** | 6 February 2026 |

## Summary

A frost period from 8 to 19 January 2026 caused an unusually high number of water damage claims.
In January 2026, 96 water damage claims were reported. A normal month has around 40.

## What happened

- From 8 to 19 January 2026, temperatures stayed well below freezing for twelve days in a row,
  mainly in the north.
- Water froze in poorly insulated pipes, for example in attics, basements, garages and
  outside walls. Frozen pipes burst.
- Most of the damage only became visible when the pipes thawed. This is why many claims were
  reported in the days during and right after the frost period.

## Effect on claims

| | Normal month | January 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Water damage claims reported | around 40 | 96 |

- Most of the January water damage claims were reported between 9 and 22 January.
- The other damage types (Storm/Hail, Fire, Glass breakage) were not affected.
- Because of the high volume, claims reported in January took a few days longer than usual.
  This is a short-term effect of the event.

## What we did

- Claims handlers from other teams supported the water damage team for three weeks.
- Drying companies were ordered as an emergency measure without waiting for the decision
  on the claim.
- Customers received a short notice explaining the longer waiting times.

## What we learned

- A frost period of more than a week leads to a peak in water damage claims with a delay of a few days.
- For the next frost warning, customers will be reminded in advance to keep unused rooms
  heated and to shut off and drain outside water pipes.
